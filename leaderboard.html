Key Takeaways
5th

Denver's National Ranking

Denver ranks 5th in the nation for DNI among major cities — ahead of San Diego, LA, Miami, Houston, Chicago, and Seattle. Only the desert Southwest beats it.

+21%

vs. Miami

Denver gets 21% more direct beam sunlight than Miami. People assume Florida = solar paradise, but humidity and clouds scatter Miami's sunlight significantly.

+7%

vs. Los Angeles

Denver even beats LA in DNI by 7%. The Mile High altitude and bone-dry air more than compensate for Denver's higher latitude and colder winters.

75%

More than Seattle

Denver gets 75% more direct sunlight than Seattle. For solar production, Denver's climate is in a completely different league from the Pacific Northwest.

5,280 ft

The Altitude Effect

At a mile high, Denver sits above ~15% of Earth's atmosphere. Less air = less scattering = more direct beam energy hitting panels. This is Denver's secret weapon.

≤ 90°F

Temperature Advantage

Solar panels lose ~0.4% efficiency per °C above 25°C. Phoenix hits 115°F regularly; Denver rarely exceeds 90°F. Cooler panels produce more electricity per unit of sunlight.
